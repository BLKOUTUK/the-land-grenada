Guest cabins · Phase 2 onward · Simon, St Andrew, Grenada

The cabins.

Distinctive hard-walled riverside cabins — a miniature of the house's own language, not a catalogue pod. Up to eight, clustered as a village on the riverbank, added only as each tranche proves it pays. Here's the design brief, the village, where they can actually be sourced (including UK makers you can visit), and the honest all-in cost of one live, lettable cabin.

The brief

Distinctive, on-brand — character in the skin, not the shell

The research was unanimous: the money is in a distinctive, story-led unit, not square footage — and character lives in the applied skin, not a costlier shell. So each cabin is a small echo of the main house: a raised, verandah-fronted planter's cottage, reinterpreted — not a grey box.

Form & skin

  • Raised on octagonal timber piers (the house's own column), deck facing the river
  • Low standing-seam pitched roof — hurricane-fixed, salt-durable
  • Whitewashed board-and-batten + one painted accent per cabin
  • Black wrought-iron hardware & lanterns; louvred Bahama shutters (shade + storm)
  • Fretwork / lattice privacy screen; a slatted outdoor shower

Experience & operations

  • Indoor–outdoor: full-height louvred doors open the river wall to the deck; vaulted inside so it reads big
  • Heated water — a small plunge/hot tub (best revenue-per-£ in the research)
  • Sited among the planting; mango shade + a stargazing gap
  • Built to let year-round: tie-downs, impact glazing, cleanable finishes, standard beds/linens
  • Warm 2700K light, cane + natural fibre, local art, one signature ritual
The village

Eight on the riverbank — clustered, so it pencils

The land carries a river along one boundary. The eight read as a village threaded along the bank, not a grid — and clustering is the whole reason eight can pay.

The platforms

  • Raised platforms/piers step the cabins down the sloping bank; each deck cantilevers to the water
  • A timber boardwalk spine links them — the arrival sequence and the privacy device in one
  • Staggered so no cabin looks into another — planting between; guests see green
  • A central commons: fire-pit + gathering deck, and the shared hot-tub deck

Shared services = the saving

  • One septic field, one solar array + battery, one water/pump line, one boardwalk — sized for 8
  • Gated build: pilot → +3 (the gate) → +4; services trenched once, cabins land as each tranche earns
  • Licensed as a villa + guest house (≤9 rooms) — a micro-village retreat, deliberately not a resort

Where to source

Three routes — visit, ship-close, or ship-cheap

1 · UK — visit before you buy

Dearest, but you can walk into one and the design quality is highest — the benchmark to hold the others against.

  • Armadilla — Livingston, Scotland; curved timber hospitality pods, en-suite. Visitable.
  • Blackdown Shepherd Huts — Somerset; premium timber, showground visit.
  • Koto — architectural A-frames & cabins, Scandi-Caribbean crossover.
  • Out of the Valley — Devon; bespoke off-grid timber cabins.

£15–60k depending on maker — dearer per unit, but inspectable, warrantied, and it holds value where China prefab depreciates. A strong candidate for the pilot, then value-engineer the fleet.

2 · Caribbean / US — hurricane-certified, closer

Higher unit cost than China, far lower shipping + risk — and a stamped wind calc for building control. The safe route for the pilot.

  • Topsider Homes — USA (since 1968); post-&-beam hurricane homes proven across the Bahamas, Antigua, the VI. Very Caribbean verandah look. ~US$175–300/sq ft.
  • Deltec Homes — N. Carolina; round homes rated to 190 mph, ship to the Caribbean. Distinctive form. ~US$250–500/sq ft.
  • Bauhu — "Caribbean Cottage" — steel-frame, hurricane-resistant, designed for Caribbean vacation-rental use.

3 · Alibaba / China — cheapest, QC first

£4–22k FOB (the seven above). Real money saved — but the experienced-operator warning stands: China prefab is only cheap if you inspect before it ships.

  • Put a sourcing agent on your side — an independent China buying agent (~5–8% commission) to vet the factory, negotiate, run the inspection and manage export + consolidation. Turns everything below into their job, not yours.
  • Independent pre-shipment inspection (SGS / TÜV, ~£250–500) — non-negotiable; witness a trial assembly.
  • Stamped wind + salt-air spec — hot-dip-galv or aluminium frame, marine fixings, a Cat-4/5 calc for GTA / building control.
  • Trade Assurance, milestone-staged (30/40/30), never 100% up front.
  • Order ONE pilot, assemble it, live with it a season — then cluster the other seven.

The calculator's £20k is really the shell + platform. The honest all-in-to-let is ~£26k on the lean China route — including a sourcing agent on your side — and £40–55k+ if you buy UK or Caribbean. At ~£26k against ~£7k net a year, a cabin still clears in ~3.7 years — the gate holds. Worth feeding the £26k back into the cabin payback in the numbers.
